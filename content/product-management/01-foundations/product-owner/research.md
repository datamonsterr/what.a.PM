# Research: Why does Scrum insist on ONE Product Owner?
Status: Validated · Updated: 2026-11-10 · Lesson: `product-management/product-owner` · Notion: (add link)

## 1. The question
In enterprises, ownership of the backlog is often shared between business, IT and compliance. I want to know why Scrum rejects that.

## 2. Assumptions I'm carrying in
- More stakeholders owning the backlog means better decisions.

## 3. Dig down

| Level | Claim | Why is it true? |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | One PO, not a committee | The Scrum Guide says so. |
| 1 | The PO orders the backlog | Order decides what gets built first. |
| 2 | Ordering is trade-offs | Doing A first means B waits. |
| ground | Capacity is finite; value is judged by customers | FP-001, FP-002 |

## 4. Ground reached
- Existing: FP-001, FP-002.

## 5. Build back up
Ordering is a constant stream of trade-offs between stakeholders. Without one accountable person the trade-offs are negotiated each time, slowly and inconsistently. So Scrum makes one person accountable and lets them delegate work.

New derived principle: **FP-008 One person is accountable for value trade-offs**. Built on: FP-001, FP-002.

## 6. Sources and validation

| Source | Type | Credibility | Date | Cross-checked with | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| [The 2020 Scrum Guide](https://scrumguides.org/scrum-guide.html) | primary | Written by Scrum's creators, Schwaber & Sutherland | Nov 2020 | Scrum.org and PSPO study guides quote the same accountability wording | use |

## 7. Limits and counter-arguments
- In large enterprises one PO can become a bottleneck; scaled frameworks add Product Managers or a Chief PO above team POs. That changes who orders which backlog, not the single-accountability idea.
