# Research: Why do customers actually buy a product?
Status: Validated · Updated: 2026-11-10 · Lesson: `product-management/jobs-to-be-done` · Notion: (add link)

## 1. The question
Stakeholders bring feature requests. I want a reliable way to understand the need behind them.

## 2. Assumptions I'm carrying in
- Customers know what they want if you ask them.
- Segments by age, income or role explain behaviour.

## 3. Dig down

| Level | Claim | Why is it true? |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | Describe jobs, not features | Features are guesses about a need. |
| 1 | People buy to make progress in a circumstance | Milkshake case: same product, different job by time of day. |
| ground | Value is decided by the customer; our ideas are often wrong | FP-002, FP-003 |

## 4. Ground reached
- Existing: FP-002, FP-003.

## 5. Build back up
Customers "hire" products to make progress in a circumstance. Profiles correlate with buying but don't cause it. So start from the job and its circumstance, and treat every feature (theirs and ours) as a hypothesis.

New derived principle: **FP-007 Describe needs as jobs, not features**. Built on: FP-002, FP-003.

## 7. Limits and counter-arguments
- Different JTBD schools (Christensen/Moesta "switch" interviews vs Ulwick's Outcome-Driven Innovation) define "job" differently. This lesson uses Christensen's.
- Jobs are hard to verify without interviews; the format can become a rewording exercise.
